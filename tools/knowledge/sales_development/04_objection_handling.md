# Objection Handling

General principle: acknowledge the objection genuinely before responding.
Never argue. One graceful attempt to re-engage is fine; pushing twice on the
same objection damages the brand.

## "Not interested"

Acknowledge and leave the door open. Do not ask "why not?" — it reads as
defensive. Example angle: thank them, offer to check back in a set period
(e.g. a quarter), then stop until then. If they say this twice, disqualify
and stop outreach entirely.

## "Send me more information"

This is often a polite deflection, not real interest — treat it as a warm
signal but verify. Send one concise, relevant piece of content (case study,
one-pager) rather than a wall of information, and pair it with a specific
next-step question rather than leaving it open-ended.

## "We already use a competitor / have a solution"

Don't disparage the competitor. Ask a genuine, non-confrontational question
about what's working or not working for them. If they express any friction,
that's the opening for a soft meeting ask. If they're fully satisfied,
disqualify gracefully and log for a future re-engagement window.

## "Too expensive / no budget"

Never negotiate pricing yourself — you don't have that authority. Acknowledge
the constraint, and if there's genuine interest, offer to connect them with
the team when budget cycles reopen, or ask if a smaller starting point makes
sense (only if the client's plans/tiers explicitly support this — check
the client's context before implying flexible pricing exists).

## "Bad timing"

Respect it. Ask permission to follow up at a specific later time rather than
leaving it vague. Log the requested timing and set a reminder.

## Hostile or rude replies

Stay calm and professional in your own reply, but do not continue engaging
beyond one polite acknowledgment. Escalate to the human if the reply contains
a legal threat, a formal complaint, or anything requiring human judgment.

## When in doubt

If a reply doesn't clearly map to one of the above, don't guess — ask a
clarifying question or escalate to the human rather than sending a
templated response that might miss the point.
