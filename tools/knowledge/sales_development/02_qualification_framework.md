# Qualification Framework

Use a lightweight BANT-style check before investing outreach time or booking
a meeting. You don't need a perfect score on all four — use judgment — but
you must be able to justify why a lead is worth pursuing.

## The four checks

**Budget** — Is there any signal the company/person can afford the client's
product? Company size, funding stage, job title (decision-maker vs. intern),
or explicit statements in their reply.

**Authority** — Is this person a decision-maker, an influencer, or a
gatekeeper? All three are worth engaging, but the message and the ask differ:
- Decision-maker → ask directly for a meeting.
- Influencer → offer to send a short case study they can forward internally.
- Gatekeeper → be polite and ask who the right contact is.

**Need** — Does their public info (LinkedIn, company website, job postings,
recent news) suggest a real problem the client's product solves? Don't
qualify someone in just because they fit a firmographic filter — look for an
actual signal of pain.

**Timing** — Any signal of urgency? Recent funding round, leadership change,
hiring spree in a relevant department, a public complaint about a competitor,
end-of-quarter budget flush, etc.

## Scoring shortcut

- **Hot (3-4 checks strong)** → prioritize outreach, personalize heavily,
  aim directly for a meeting ask.
- **Warm (1-2 checks strong)** → standard sequence, nurture over multiple
  touches before asking for a meeting.
- **Cold / no signal** → deprioritize or skip. Don't waste sequence slots on
  leads with zero fit signal just to hit a volume number.

## Disqualifying signals — stop pursuing

- Explicit "not interested, don't contact again."
- Company is a direct competitor of the client.
- Company is clearly too small/large for the client's stated ideal customer
  profile (check the client's business context for ICP details).
- Contact has left the company (bounced email, outdated LinkedIn role).

## What "qualified" means for a meeting handoff

Before booking a meeting for a human, you should be able to state in one
sentence: who this is, what problem they likely have, and why now. If you
can't fill in all three, don't book yet — send one more qualifying question
first.
