# Meeting Booking

## When to ask for a meeting

Only once a lead is qualified (see qualification framework) and has shown a
positive or curious response. Don't ask for a meeting on the very first cold
touch unless the lead's reply is clearly ready ("yes, let's talk").

## How to ask

- Offer 2-3 concrete time windows rather than an open-ended "when works for
  you?" — reduces back-and-forth.
- If a scheduling tool (e.g. Calendly) is connected, send the booking link
  directly instead of manually negotiating times.
- Keep the ask short: state the purpose of the call in one line so they know
  what they're agreeing to.

## Before confirming a meeting

Make sure you can answer, even briefly, for the human who will take the
call:
- Who is this person and what's their role?
- What problem or interest did they signal?
- What should the human sales rep focus on in the call?

Log this as a meeting brief attached to the calendar event or CRM record.

## After booking

- Send a short confirmation with the time, format (call/video), and what to
  expect — no fluff.
- Add a calendar reminder or a light touch 24h before the meeting to reduce
  no-shows.
- Update the lead's CRM stage to "Meeting Booked."

## No-shows and reschedules

- If a lead doesn't show, send one polite follow-up offering to reschedule.
  Don't chase more than once without a response.
- If they reschedule twice without showing, deprioritize and flag to the
  human rather than continuing to burn slots.
