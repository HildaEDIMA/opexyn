# Lead Sourcing (when no CRM or lead list exists)

If the client has no CRM, no imported lead list, and no existing pipeline,
you are responsible for building your own pipeline from scratch using your
browser tools and the client's Ideal Customer Profile (ICP) — found in their
business context (business_description, business_priorities, goals). If the
ICP isn't explicit, infer it conservatively from the business description
before sourcing anything, and log your inferred ICP as a memory so it stays
consistent across sessions.

## Where to look (in priority order)

1. **The client's own signals first** — website visitors, existing customer
   list patterns, competitors' public customer lists (case studies pages),
   industry associations/directories relevant to their niche.
2. **LinkedIn (public search only)** — search by job title + industry +
   company size that match the ICP. Only use publicly visible profile
   information (name, role, company, public posts). Do not attempt to access
   gated data, connect at high volume, or automate actions that resemble
   bot-like scraping patterns (see rate limits below) — LinkedIn actively
   detects and bans automated behavior, which would burn the client's
   account.
3. **Company websites and public directories** — "About us" / leadership
   pages, industry directories, Chamber of Commerce listings, startup
   databases (e.g. Crunchbase public pages), local business registries.
4. **Public company news and job postings** — hiring for a role connected to
   the client's product is a strong timing signal (see qualification
   framework's "Timing" check).

## What counts as fair game vs. off-limits

- **Fair game**: publicly published business contact info (company email
  formats, public LinkedIn profiles, public company directories, published
  press releases).
- **Off-limits**: purchased/leaked data lists, personal (non-business) email
  addresses, scraping behind logins or paywalls, circumventing platform
  rate limits or anti-bot protections, inferring or guessing personal contact
  info beyond standard business email patterns.

## Rate limits and platform safety

- Space out LinkedIn searches and profile views — treat it like a human
  would browse, not a script. Bursts of activity are what gets accounts
  flagged.
- Cap sourcing activity so it doesn't crowd out qualification, outreach, and
  follow-ups — sourcing is one part of the day, not the whole day (see daily
  planning doc).
- If a platform blocks, rate-limits, or shows a CAPTCHA, stop immediately
  and log it — do not try to bypass it. Report the blockage to the human.

## Compliance — US & EU cold outreach

The client operates in both the US and Europe, so apply the stricter EU
standard by default unless the client's context says otherwise:

- **B2B cold email in the EU** is generally permitted under "legitimate
  interest" (GDPR) when the outreach is relevant to the recipient's
  professional role — but every email must include a clear way to opt out,
  and any opt-out or "remove me" request must be honored immediately and
  permanently (add to a suppression list, never contact again).
- **Do not source or store more personal data than necessary** — name, role,
  company, business email, and public professional context is enough. Don't
  collect or retain personal (non-work) details.
- **US (CAN-SPAM)** — must include a valid sender identity, a physical or
  registered business address if required by the client's setup, and a
  working opt-out mechanism.
- If the client's plan/rules do not clarify these requirements, flag it as a
  setup gap rather than assuming — this is a compliance risk for the client,
  not just a style choice.

## Turning a sourced contact into a pipeline lead

Once you've found a contact:
1. Run them through the qualification framework before spending outreach
   effort.
2. Log them as a new lead with source noted ("sourced: LinkedIn search,
   [date]") so the human always knows where a contact came from.
3. Enter them into the standard outreach cadence like any other lead.
